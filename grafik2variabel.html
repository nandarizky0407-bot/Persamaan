Core Concept

Drawing Graphs on the Cartesian Plane

A Cartesian plane has two perpendicular axes — the horizontal x-axis and the vertical y-axis. Every point is written as an ordered pair (x, y). Here's how to graph a linear equation in three simple steps.

1

Build a table of values

Choose 4-5 x-values, substitute each into the equation, and compute the matching y-values.

2

Plot the points

Move x units right/left, then y units up/down. Mark each point clearly.

3

Draw the line

Connect the points with a straight line. Extend it across the plane and add arrows on both ends.

Identify the Graph of an Equation

Given an equation, can you spot which graph matches? Look at the y-intercept and the slope direction — they're your two biggest clues.

Which graph represents y = 2x + 1?

Tip: A positive slope (2) means the line rises left-to-right. The y-intercept (+1) means the line crosses the y-axis above the origin.

Pro Tips

  • Use easy x-values: −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 are quick to compute.
  • Always include x = 0: that gives you the y-intercept directly.
  • Plot at least 3 points: if they line up, your equation is correct.
  • Extend the line: a linear equation has infinitely many points — don't stop at the table.
  • Use a ruler: neatness matters when communicating mathematics.

Interpret a Graph: Word Problems

Graphs tell stories. A distance-time graph can show a journey — when someone walks fast, slow, or stops to rest. Learn to read the story behind the line.

Maya's Walk to School

Maya walks to school. The graph shows her distance from home over time.

Red dots mark key moments in the journey

Reading the Story

0 → 5 min: Steep uphill line means Maya walks quickly. She covers a lot of distance in a short time.
5 → 10 min: Flat horizontal line means her distance doesn't change. She stopped to rest or wait.
10 → 15 min: Gentle uphill line means she walks again, but more slowly than before.
Steepness = speed: The steeper the line, the faster the movement. Flat sections mean no movement.

Write an Equation from a Graph Using a Table

Reverse engineer the equation. Read points off the graph, build a table, then figure out what equation could produce those values.

1 Read points from the graph

Pick 3 clear points where the line crosses grid intersections.

2 Build a table

x-202
y 123

From x = 0, we read the y-intercept: b = 2

Slope: m = Δy/Δx = (3−1)/(2−(−2)) = 2/4 = ½

3 Write the equation

Substitute m and b into y = mx + b:

y = ½x + 2

Verify with point (2, 3): ½(2) + 2 = 1 + 2 = 3 ✓
